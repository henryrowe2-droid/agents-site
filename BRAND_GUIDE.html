Typography

Three faces, each with one job.

Instrument Serif
Display · 400 · roman and italic
Handling homes, not hype.
Every headline on the site. Never used below 24px and never for body copy. The italic is the emphasis device: wrap a phrase in it and set it in Accent. One emphasis per heading, never two.
Inter
Body · 400 500 600 700
A spreadsheet of names is a starting line, not a pipeline. Short sentences carry weight. Long ones carry nuance. Alternate.
All reading copy, buttons, navigation and labels. 400 for body, 600 for anything that has to be found while scanning. 700 is rare.
JetBrains Mono
Utility · 400 500
Free guide · No card, no email
Eyebrows, step numbers, price chips, kickers, and any block of text the reader is meant to copy. Always uppercase with wide tracking when used as a label.

The mark

A square with the middle removed.

A rounded square filled with the Accent gradient, with a smaller rounded square knocked out of the centre in whatever colour sits behind it. There is no wordmark file. The mark always sits beside the words "Agents for Agents" set in Inter 600.

Agents for Agents
01
The knockout takes the background colour On paper it is #faf8f5. On an ink band it is #16151a. It is never white by default, which is what makes the mark sit in the page rather than on it.
02
Corner radius scales with the mark Roughly 28 percent of the width on the outer square, 27 percent on the knockout. A 24px mark uses a 7px radius. A 180px mark uses 50px.
03
Clear space is one knockout width Nothing enters that margin. In the navigation the gap to the wordmark is 12px at 24px mark size.
04
Never recolour the gradient 135 degrees, Accent to Accent 2. Not flat, not reversed, not a third colour. If the background cannot hold it, use the mark on an ink or paper plate instead.

04
Selling inside a page

A paid offer must break the page, not continue it.

The single most important rule in this system. A reader scrolling past body copy must never be able to mistake an offer for another paragraph. Six components, in ascending loudness. Pick the quietest one that will still be seen.

1
.ptag
A price chip inside a route list heading. Turns a list of links into a priced menu. Quietest possible signal that something costs money.
2
.plug
Compact tinted band with a left rule. Used once, mid way through a long free guide, to say a paid version exists without hijacking the guide.
3
.offers .offer
Three card product grid with a gradient top edge. Ends most pages. Use .hot on the one you want chosen.
4
.offer-wide
One product promoted mid page, copy left and price right. For when a single offer deserves the room but not the full band.
5
.pitch
Full bleed ink band with a gradient top edge. One per page, placed at the moment the reader is most convinced. Must sit outside section and outside wrap.
6
.pitch-xl
The flagship. Full width head, the artifact shown as a mock, then the arithmetic that makes the price feel small next to what it replaces.

The pattern that does the selling is always the same: state the number plainly, then immediately put it next to what it replaces. Never justify a price with adjectives.

05
Voice

Agent to agent. Never guru.

Henry is a practicing agent who built these systems for his own book and then packaged them. Every line on every page has to sound like it came from someone who still takes listing appointments.

01
No dashes. Ever. No em dashes, no en dashes, no double hyphens, anywhere, including page titles, markup and commit messages. Commas, colons and periods only. Titles use a middle dot.
02
No hype vocabulary Not game changer, unlock, 10x, revolutionary, or supercharge. If a sentence needs one of those words to land, the claim underneath it is not strong enough.
03
Sell the outcome, withhold the mechanism A visitor should finish an offer page convinced it works and still not know exactly how it is built. Free guide pages are the deliberate exception: those teach the thing completely.
04
Every number is real, with its assumptions shown No income claims, no guarantees, no invented testimonials or logos, no fake urgency. A figure without its arithmetic beside it does not go on the page.
05
Compliance is a selling point Fair housing, Do Not Call, opt out, and "nothing sends without you" get stated plainly wherever they are relevant. They are the reason to buy, not a disclaimer.

One stylesheet. One spec.

Nothing in this document is maintained by hand. It describes files that already run the site, and those files are the authority if the two ever disagree.
