Before you start

Your clients can tell. They just do not say anything.

Nobody replies to an email to tell you it sounded like a machine. They just answer a little slower, and a little shorter, and you never find out why.

Telling Claude to write in a "professional but friendly tone" does not fix it, because that instruction produces the average of everyone who has ever typed it. The average is exactly what sounds fake. The only thing that works is showing it how you actually write and having it keep that on file.

That is all this setup is. Ten minutes, one time, and every piece of writing after it starts from you instead of from nobody.

The setup

Five steps. Do them in order.

Everything below happens at claude.ai in a browser. You do not need Claude Code, an extension, or anything installed.

01

Collect five things you already wrote

Open your sent folder and your texts. Copy three emails you sent to clients and two texts. Not your best work, your ordinary work. Something you fired off in ninety seconds beats something you agonized over, because the fast one is actually how you sound. Paste all five into a blank note so they are ready in one place.

02

Open claude.ai and start a new chat

Go to claude.ai in your browser and sign in. Click New chat in the top left. Do not use an existing conversation, because whatever you were talking about before will bleed into the result.

03

Paste the samples and ask the right question

Paste all five samples into the chat. Then ask it to analyze what is distinctive about how you write, not what is good about it. That single word is the whole trick. Ask for the specifics: sentence length, whether you use contractions, how you open, how you sign off, the phrases you lean on, and what you never do. Tell it to ignore anything that would be true of any competent writer.

04

Push back once, then have it written as rules

Read what it gives you. Some of it will be wrong. Tell it which parts are not you and let it correct itself. Then ask it to turn the result into a short set of written rules you can reuse, phrased as instructions rather than as a description. Copy that block of text. That is your voice profile, and it is the only artifact you need from this whole process.

05

Save it where Claude will always see it

Click your name in the bottom left, then Settings, then Profile. There is a field for personal preferences that Claude applies to every conversation. Paste your voice profile in there and save. If you are on a plan with Projects, you can also create a Project for your real estate work and paste it into the project instructions, which keeps it out of your personal chats.

Prove it worked

Run the same request twice. You will hear it.

Do not take my word for it, and do not take Claude's. Take two minutes and check.

Ask for a follow up email to a buyer who went quiet, in a chat where the profile is off.
You will get the assistant. Smooth, polite, three tidy sentences, and a closing line you would never actually type.
Ask for the identical thing with your profile saved.
You will get something you would send. Maybe not perfect, but recognizably yours, and editable in fifteen seconds instead of rewritten from scratch.
Send one of each to a person who knows you well and ask which you wrote.
This is the real test. If they cannot tell, you are done. If they pick correctly every time, go back to step three with better samples.

Worth knowing

What this does not do.

  • It captures how you write, not what you know. Your market knowledge still has to come from you
  • It will not save a bad message. A wrong idea in your voice is still wrong
  • You still read everything. Nothing should reach a client that you have not looked at
  • Redact client details before pasting samples. Names, addresses and numbers do not need to be in there
  • Refresh it once a year. How you write drifts, and the profile should drift with it
  • One profile per hat. How you write to a seller and how you write to another agent are different jobs
